Hunger is often an access problem

Amartya Sen's Entitlement Theory

"Hunger is often caused not by lack of food, but by lack of access to food."

Sen's theory shows that food insecurity can occur even when food is available, because people may lose access through poverty, inflation, conflict, market failure or weak social support.

Mini case study

UK Food Banks

Even in high-income countries, food insecurity can persist. In the UK, rising food prices and cost-of-living pressures have increased reliance on food banks, showing that food access depends on income, affordability and social support — not only national food supply.

No single solution is enough

Can food systems become more sustainable without reducing affordability and productivity?

Sustainable Intensification

Strength: Improves efficiency and yield.

Limitation: May still depend on industrial inputs.

Agroecology

Strength: Supports ecological resilience.

Limitation: May face short-term yield or scaling challenges.

Food Aid & Social Protection

Strength: Provides immediate relief.

Limitation: May not solve structural inequality.

Food Sovereignty & Policy Reform

Strength: Empowers local communities and addresses structural access.

Limitation: Difficult to implement within existing global free-trade systems.

No response solves all dimensions of food insecurity simultaneously.

Food security requires responses that address production, access, nutrition, resilience, and sustainability together.

Food security is not simply about producing more food.

It is about access, equity, resilience, and sustainability.

The challenge is not only how to grow more food, but how to build a food system that feeds people fairly and sustainably.
